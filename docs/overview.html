Shorts system

One podcast episode in. Finished vertical Shorts in a private review app out. You approve what goes live. Every note you give becomes a rule.

What it does

It reads the transcript and finds passages that stand alone. It scores them, writes the hooks, cuts each one in sync, and draws motion graphics that land on the spoken word. Then it checks every frame by machine. Only what passes reaches you.

The whole flow

  1. Recording Riverside or local files
  2. Transcript words with numbers
  3. Pick score out of 22
  4. Hook spoken and written
  5. Cut one take, in sync
  6. Motion beats land on words
  7. Render one renderer
  8. Gates must all pass
  9. Review app you watch and note
  10. Approve your click only
  11. Schedule Buffer, own time
  12. Posted your platforms
  13. Stats real numbers
  14. Notes into rules R200 and up
  • Claude builds
  • Machine checks
  • You decide
  • Posting
  • Feedback

A failed gate goes back to the build. It never goes to you (R102). A note in the app goes back to the rules, so the next Short starts better.

The rules

  • RULES.md holds R1 to R125. Each one came from a real note or from the research. Each says what to do, why, and what checks it.
  • rules/MY-RULES.md holds yours, from R200.
  • Type /shorts-rule with a note like "captions too small". You get a rule, a code gate if it can be measured, a check for clashes with older rules, and a plain report.
  • If two rules clash, the outlier data settles it, not taste (R84).
  • The research in research/ is correlation from about 5,200 Shorts on 54 podcast channels, mostly tech and business. Test it in your niche.

Your brand

brand/brand.json is the one source for your look. /shorts-setup asks you questions and writes it.

  • Show: name, audience, topics, your saturated topic, voice, banned words.
  • People: 1, 2 or 3 hosts. Solo works.
  • Look: colours, fonts, logo, texture style, signature motion move, title and caption settings, end card.
  • Posting: platforms, time zone, gaps between posts, tags.

setup/build_brand.py turns that file into CSS, fonts, texture plates and sounds for every part of the system. You see a preview and approve it. Nothing renders until you do. To change your look later, edit the file and build again.

You bring your own music, LUT, fonts and logo, and you own the rights to them. Fair use is not assumed for anything you put on screen.

How approval works, status by status, is in docs/approval.md.
