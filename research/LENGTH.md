# How long can a Short be?

Data: 5,236 podcast Shorts, 3 to 90 days old, on public channels. Multiple = views / that channel's median. Correlation only. Raw data not included.

| length | n | 5x+ rate | 10x+ rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20-30 s | 679 | 10.3% | 2.9% |
| 30-45 s | 1559 | 6.9% | 2.0% |
| 45-60 s | 1291 | 6.0% | 2.9% |
| 60-75 s | 677 | 5.3% | 2.2% |
| 75-90 s | 367 | 6.3% | 3.5% |
| 90-120 s | 309 | 14.9% | 5.5% |
| 120-180 s | 151 | 19.9% | 9.9% |

Remote or two-host tech shows only: 120-180 s = 21.0% at 5x+, against 4.0 to 4.4% for 30-75 s.
Without Alex Hormozi (the biggest poster of long Shorts): 90 s+ still 12.7% at 5x+.

**But:** inside each channel, long beats short in only 10 of 20 channels. Long wins clearly at Hard Fork (2.7x against 0.85x), Dwarkesh (3.3x against 0.47x), Pivot (4.1x against 0.96x), The Standup Clips (1.8x against 0.87x) and Diary of a CEO (1.5x against 0.55x). It loses at My First Million, TBPN, Bankless and Every.

So length does not cause a win. A long Short wins when the passage is one complete story that keeps paying off.

**Rule R32:** no 60 s cap. Length follows the story: up to 3 minutes when the passage is one take with a payoff every 15 to 20 s. Never pad. A 30 s Short that is the whole story beats a 90 s one that drifts.
